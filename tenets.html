Dan Karlin

Work
Tenets

What I've come to believe about doing good work — and helping others do it.

Scroll ↓
01
First and foremost, be helpful. If you focus primarily on the success of others through the conduct of your role, you're highly likely to succeed and for that to be evident to everyone involved.
02
Say less than you know. Depth shows in the quality of what you do, not the volume of what you say.
03
Always understand the system you're inside. Know its assumptions, know its limits, and you'll know when it's leading you somewhere that won't hold.
04
Never worry alone.
05
It doesn't matter to anybody else where or when you work if your work is excellent and you're helpful. It means you were doing what you needed to do and were where you needed to be when you needed to be there.
06
Say what you're going to do and do what you said you would.
07
Systems and vendors work for us, not the other way around. If either is getting in the way, fix it, replace it, or do what it does better. Always look for opportunities to reduce friction: automate, eliminate, simplify.
08
No long toes. If you feel like your toes are getting stepped on a lot, that's probably on you. Offers to help should be accepted at face value. Find ways to let others help you as you do your best to help them. Getting things done well as a team is what matters.
09
Evaluative and formative feedback are hard to do at once. If evaluative feedback comes as a surprise to the recipient, the formative feedback has been lacking or their self-awareness is off. Often it's both.
10
Always better to ask and know than not to ask. Whether it's a research question or interpersonal strife, if you find yourself not wanting to risk knowing the answer, it's exactly the time to ask the question.
11
Match the time frame of the solution to the time scale of the problem. If the building is on fire, grab a hose or a bucket. Don't debate fire codes. If the building keeps catching on fire, make the time between fires to fix the code and stop the fires. If there's no time between fires, hire firefighters and elevate yourself.
12
Real risk management makes things possible safely. In any growing organization, some functions drift toward measuring their value by how many things they've slowed or stopped on the basis of theoretical risk. It doesn't treat caution as an end in itself. If a function can't take the people it serves to a shared view of reality, that needs immediate repair.
13
Good leaders know when to lead and when to get out of the way — and do both repeatedly. The case for following is made through trust, ideas, and action, not by asserting authority. Those who demand power rarely deserve it; those who demonstrate judgment rarely need to ask for it.
14
When speaking to groups as a leader, invert inflection between tone and topic. It creates consistency while allowing you to match the levity or gravity of whatever you're facing together.
15
Defining targets is important, but equally so is noticing what you're hitting. There are times for drawing bullseyes before you throw the darts and times for circling clusters of darts. Know which is which and how to pivot.
16
Strategy doesn't mean sticking to a decision you made when you knew less. You always knew less yesterday than you do today. Balancing adaptability to new knowledge and stability that allows people to confidently move forward is critical.
17
There are lots of right ways to do the right thing and no right way to do the wrong thing.
